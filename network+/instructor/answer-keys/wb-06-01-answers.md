**Category cheat:** Cat5e = 1 Gbps/100 m · Cat6 = 1 Gbps/100 m (10 Gbps ≤55 m) · Cat6a = 10 Gbps/100 m · Cat7 = 10 Gbps shielded · Cat8 = 25–40 Gbps/≤30 m.

## Quiz 6.1.Q — Knowledge Check (10 pts)

1. **C** — 8 copper wires (4 twisted pairs).
2. **B** — Twisting cancels interference (noise hits both wires and cancels).
3. **B** — UTP = Unshielded Twisted Pair.
4. **B** — STP adds a metal shield to block interference.
5. **B** — Cat6a supports full 10 Gbps at 100 m.
6. **False.** Good answer: the cable sets a *ceiling*; a slow plan/gear stays slow no matter the cable.
7. **C** — 100 m is the standard twisted-pair run limit.
8. Cat5e → **B**, Cat6a → **A**, Cat8 → **C**.
9. **crosstalk**.
10. **Cat6a** (10 Gbps at up to 100 m covers 90 m). Not Cat5e because Cat5e tops out at 1 Gbps.

**Scoring:** 1 pt each. Pass = 8/10 (80%).

## Worksheet 6.1.D — Short answer (20 pts)

1. Four twisted pairs of copper wire (8 wires) inside a jacket. *(idea /3, why /2)*
2. Twisting makes noise hit both wires equally and cancel, so the signal stays clean. *(idea /3, why /2)*
3. UTP has no shield (cheap, common); STP adds a metal shield — choose STP in noisy areas (near motors/machines). *(idea /3, why /2)*
4. The cable only sets the top speed (a ceiling); the plan and gear set the real speed. *(idea /3, why /2)*
