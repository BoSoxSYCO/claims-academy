## Quiz answers (`quiz-00-03-knowledge-check.md`)

1. **B — answering from memory before checking.** That is active recall. *(Re-reading, copying, or skipping are not.)*

2. **B — it feels familiar but fades fast.** Recognition is not the same as recall.

3. **B — reviewing again after growing gaps of time.** That is spaced repetition. *(Cramming once is the opposite.)*

4. **A — 15–25 minutes, focused.** Short, focused sessions beat marathons.

5. **B — short and in your own words.** Putting it in your words builds understanding.

6. **False.** Familiarity is not knowledge — you can recognize a page and still fail to recall it; always test yourself.

7. **A — the quiz.** The quiz (and the workbook) make you answer from memory.

8. **Matching:** Active recall → B (answering from memory); Spaced repetition → A (reviews at growing gaps); Notes → C (ideas in your own words).

9. **cram.** A little often beats one long cram.

10. **Scenario —** Any two of: recall the lesson from memory then check; write a one-sentence summary in your own words; schedule a spaced review in a few days; do the workbook and quiz; add missed points to a weak-spots list for flashcards later. *(Accept any two real habits from the lesson.)*

### Short answer (`wb-00-03-short-answer.md`) — model answers
1. Your eyes glide over familiar words, so it feels productive but the memory fades quickly.
2. Answering from memory before checking; read a bit, look away, say it, then check.
3. Reviewing after growing time gaps; each spaced review strengthens the memory and fights forgetting.
4. Rewriting in your own words forces understanding; copying is passive and doesn't stick.
5. About 15–25 focused minutes, most days.
6. The workbook worksheets and the quiz (and later the flashcards).
7. Any reasonable rhythm: a short daily session with a one-sentence summary, plus a weekly recall pass.

### Vocabulary review (`wb-00-03-vocabulary-review.md`)
- **Active recall** — answering from memory before checking; the strongest way to learn.
- **Spaced repetition** — reviewing again after growing gaps of time so it sticks.
- **Flashcard** — a card with a question on one side and the answer on the other.
