## Quiz 6.5.Q — Knowledge Check (10 pts)

1. **B** — Ethernet is the family of rules for wired local networks (802.3).
2. **B** — "1000" means 1000 Mbps (1 Gbps).
3. **B** — "T" means twisted-pair copper.
4. **C** — 1000BASE-T is Gigabit Ethernet.
5. **D** — 10GBASE-T needs Cat6a for a full 100 m run.
6. **False.** Good answer: it runs only as fast as the slowest part — if the switch does 100 Mbps, the link is 100 Mbps.
7. **A** — Auto-negotiation picks the fastest speed and duplex both devices support.
8. 100BASE-TX → **C**, 1000BASE-T → **A**, 10GBASE-T → **B**.
9. **duplex** (full-duplex).
10. **100 Mbps** — the link runs at the slowest part; the 100 Mbps switch port is the limit.

**Scoring:** 1 pt each. Pass = 8/10 (80%).

## Worksheet 6.5.D — Short answer (20 pts)

1. Read left to right: number = speed (Mbps), BASE = normal Ethernet, T = copper. *(idea /3, why /2)*
2. 100 Mbps → Cat5+; 1 Gbps → Cat5e+; 10 Gbps → Cat6a. *(idea /3, why /2)*
3. Auto-negotiation lets two devices agree on the fastest speed/duplex both support, with no manual setup. *(idea /3, why /2)*
4. The link runs at the slowest part; e.g., a gigabit laptop on a 100 Mbps port runs at 100 Mbps. *(idea /3, why /2)*
