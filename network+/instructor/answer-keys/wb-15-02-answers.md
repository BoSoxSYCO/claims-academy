## Quiz answers (`quiz-15-02-knowledge-check.md`)

1. **B — 720 out of 900.** The pass mark is 720 on a 100–900 scale. *(It is not a simple 70% or a perfect score.)*

2. **B — 90 minutes.** The exam is timed at 90 minutes.

3. **A — a hands-on task, like a drag-and-drop.** A PBQ is a simulation or interactive task, not a plain question. *(It is not true/false, essay, or spoken.)*

4. **B — flag it, do fast questions first, return later.** Bank the easy points before spending time on a slow PBQ. *(Grinding on it early or quitting both waste points.)*

5. **B — guess your best answer on each.** There is no penalty for guessing, so a guess can only help. *(A blank is a guaranteed zero.)*

6. **False.** It is a scaled score (100–900), not a raw percentage — the questions are weighted and converted, so 720 is not "72% right."

7. **B — a valid government photo ID.** You must prove your identity to test. *(No laptop, keyboard, or calculator is needed or allowed.)*

8. **Matching:** **Voucher → B** (prepaid code for your seat); **Scaled score → A** (the 100–900 converted score); **Pearson VUE → C** (delivers the exam).

9. **blank.** Never leave a question blank — always guess.

10. **Scenario —** Do the **five fast multiple-choice items first** (guess if unsure — quick, near-sure points), then spend the rest on the **two PBQs**, and make sure **nothing is left blank** before submitting. Fast points per minute are higher on multiple-choice than on slow PBQs. *(Accept any answer that banks the fast items first and leaves no blanks.)*

### Short answer (`wb-15-02-short-answer.md`) — model answers
1. 720 out of 900.
2. The questions are weighted and converted to a 100–900 scale, so the number is not a plain percent of items correct.
3. A hands-on task (drag-and-drop, matching, or a simulated screen); it takes longer because you interact with it, not just pick one choice.
4. So you bank the many fast points first; a slow PBQ early can eat time you need for easy questions.
5. There is no penalty for guessing, so a guess can score while a blank is always zero.
6. A valid government photo ID and your exam voucher (seat).
7. A quick list of facts you fear forgetting (ports, OSI layers), written on the scratch material right when you sit down.
