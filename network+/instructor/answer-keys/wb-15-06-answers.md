### Short answer (`wb-15-06-short-answer.md`) — model answers
1. It's passive — your eyes skim familiar words while the hard parts stay hard, and time runs out.
2. Answering from memory before checking; it forces retrieval, which builds stronger memory than re-reading.
3. Reviewing after growing time gaps; the spacing fights forgetting and locks facts in for the long term.
4. When you can answer it cold and correctly, twice, spaced apart — not once, not while looking.
5. A correct answer moves it up a box; a miss sends it back to Box 1 for daily review.
6. So you practice recall (pulling the answer), not just recognition (seeing it and thinking "I knew that").
7. Spacing beats cramming for memory, and short sessions keep focus high and fatigue low.

### Vocabulary review (`wb-15-06-vocabulary-review.md`)
- **Active recall** — answering from memory before checking; the strongest way to learn.
- **Spaced repetition** — reviewing a fact after growing gaps of time so it sticks.
- **Flashcard** — a card with a question on one side and the answer on the other.
- **Baseline** — an earlier score/weak-spot set to measure improvement against.

**Part 2 (three steps):** Prioritize · Drill · Confirm.
**Part 3 (Leitner):** Box 1 = daily · Box 2 = every few days · Box 3 = weekly.
**Closed when:** you answer it cold and correctly, twice, spaced apart.
