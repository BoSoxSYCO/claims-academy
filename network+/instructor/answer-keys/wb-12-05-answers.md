## Quiz answers (`quiz-12-05-knowledge-check.md`)

1. **B — a written rule for how to do something.** A policy is a rule you can point to and prove. *(A is a cable, C is a routing protocol, D is a backup — none are rules.)*

2. **A — what people may and may not do with the network.** That is exactly an Acceptable Use Policy. *(B/C/D are settings or hardware, not use rules.)*

3. **B — rules like length and complexity for passwords.** A password policy sets length, character mix, and change rules. *(A/C/D are unrelated network settings.)*

4. **B — removing a leaver's access and collecting gear.** Offboarding is the exit process. *(A is onboarding, the opposite; C and D are unrelated.)*

5. **A — personal devices on the network.** BYOD = Bring Your Own Device. *(B/C/D are other topics.)*

6. **False.** Being friendly does not remove someone's access — you must always offboard so no open door is left behind, no matter how the person parted.

7. **B — a promise not to share private information.** An NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement) protects confidential information. *(A/C/D are network things, not promises.)*

8. **Matching:** **SLA → B** (a service-quality promise, like uptime); **MOU → C** (an informal agreement to work together); **SOW → A** (a detailed list of the work to be done).

9. **Onboarding.** Setting up a new employee's accounts and access is onboarding.

10. **Scenario —** Two things failed: (1) the **offboarding** process was skipped, and (2) the **remote-access policy** (and account controls) let an ex-worker still log in from home. What should have happened: on the worker's last day, all their accounts, passwords, VPN access, and keys should have been disabled or removed, and any lab/company gear collected. Removing access the day someone leaves is the key best practice.

### Short answer (`wb-12-05-short-answer.md`) — model answers
1. A policy is a written rule for doing something safely and fairly; networks need them to protect data, stay fair, keep everyone consistent, and prove the rule in an audit.
2. An AUP covers what people may and may not do with the network. Example: "no sharing your password" or "no illegal downloads."
3. Onboarding sets up a new person's accounts and access; offboarding removes a leaver's access and collects their gear.
4. Skipping offboarding leaves an ex-employee with working access — an open door an attacker (or the ex-employee) can use.
5. An NDA is a promise not to share private or confidential information; new staff often sign one when they join.
6. An MOU is an informal "we agree to work together" note; an SOW is a detailed list of exactly what work will be done.
7. A data-handling (retention) policy decides how to store, protect, and eventually delete data, and how long records are kept.

### Hands-on observation (`wb-12-05-hands-on-observation.md`)
Answers vary. Full credit when: the password policy has **three specific** rules (e.g., "at least 12 characters," "mix of letters/numbers/symbols," "change if exposed"); the AUP has **three** clear rules (e.g., "no sharing passwords," "no unknown downloads," "report problems"); and the offboarding step names a real action (e.g., "wipe the device and remove its access"). Reject vague rules like "be strong" or "be safe."

### Vocabulary review (`wb-12-05-vocabulary-review.md`)
- **Policy** — a written rule for doing something safely and fairly.
- **AUP** — what people may and may not do with the network.
- **Password policy** — rules for password length, complexity, and changes.
- **BYOD** — rules for personal devices on the network.
- **Remote-access policy** — rules for connecting from outside (often a VPN).
- **Onboarding** — setting up a new person's accounts and access.
- **Offboarding** — removing a leaver's access and collecting gear.
- **NDA** — a promise not to share private information.
- **Data-handling policy** — how to store, protect, and delete data (retention).
- **SLA** — a service-quality promise (like uptime).
- **MOU** — an informal agreement to work together.
- **SOW** — a detailed list of the work to be done.
