## Quiz answers (`quiz-13-02-knowledge-check.md`)

1. **B — vulnerability.** An attack happens when a threat (danger) meets a vulnerability (weak spot). *(A password and a backup are defenses; a cable is unrelated.)*

2. **B — a fake message that tricks you into giving secrets.** Phishing targets people through fake messages. *(A/C/D are hardware or methods, not tricks.)*

3. **C — availability.** Ransomware locks your files, so you cannot reach your data — that breaks availability. *(It may also touch integrity, but availability is the main hit.)*

4. **A — faking an identity to look trusted.** Spoofing pretends to be a trusted address or device. *(B/C/D are unrelated tasks.)*

5. **B — floods a service so real users cannot reach it.** A Denial of Service attack overwhelms a service. *(A is eavesdropping, C is ransomware, D is the opposite of the effect.)*

6. **False.** A threat is the danger (the burglar); a vulnerability is the weak spot it uses (the unlocked window). They are different — an attack needs both.

7. **B — confidentiality (the attacker reads the traffic).** An on-path attacker sits between two parties and can read (and sometimes change) the traffic. *(It is not limited to availability, and it does affect security.)*

8. **Matching:** **Phishing → C** (a fake message that steals secrets); **DoS → A** (floods a service offline); **Malware → B** (harmful software on a device).

9. **People (persons/users).** Social engineering targets people, not machines.

10. **Scenario —** This is a **DoS (or DDoS)** attack — fake traffic floods the site so real customers cannot load it. It broke **availability**. *(Accept "DDoS" since the flood likely comes from many machines.)*

### Short answer (`wb-13-02-short-answer.md`) — model answers
1. A threat is anything that could cause harm (the danger); a vulnerability is a weak spot that lets the harm happen. An attack needs both.
2. Phishing is a fake message that tricks you into giving up secrets. It targets people, often by looking like a real email.
3. Ransomware locks your files and demands money to unlock them. It breaks availability (you can't reach your data).
4. Spoofing is faking an identity so the attacker looks trusted (like a fake caller ID).
5. An on-path attack lets the attacker secretly sit between two parties and read — or change — their traffic.
6. A DoS comes from one source; a DDoS is the same flood from many machines at once, which is harder to stop.
7. Because tricking a person (phishing, social engineering) is often easier than beating strong technology. People are the top target.

### Vocabulary review (`wb-13-02-vocabulary-review.md`)
- **Threat** — anything that could cause harm.
- **Vulnerability** — a weak spot that lets harm happen.
- **Attack** — the harmful act itself.
- **Malware** — any harmful software.
- **Ransomware** — malware that locks files and demands money.
- **Social engineering** — tricking a person into breaking security.
- **Phishing** — a fake message that steals secrets.
- **Spoofing** — faking a trusted identity.
- **On-path attack** — secretly sitting between two parties to read or change traffic.
- **DoS** — a flood that knocks a service offline.
- **DDoS** — a DoS from many machines at once.

**Part 3 quick recall:** 1. DoS (or DDoS) · 2. social engineering (or phishing) · 3. ransomware
