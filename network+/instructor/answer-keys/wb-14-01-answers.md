## Quiz answers (`quiz-14-01-knowledge-check.md`)

1. **B — Identify the problem.** Step 1 is always to gather facts and understand the problem. *(Documenting is last; implementing and testing come later.)*

2. **B — your best guess at the cause.** A theory of probable cause is a hypothesis you test. *(A report is documentation; the fix is later; a cable is unrelated.)*

3. **C — Establish a new theory.** A failed test sends you back to step 2 for a new theory. *(Don't give up, implement a wrong theory, or stop.)*

4. **B — pass it to someone with more access or skill.** Escalation routes the problem to the right person. *(It doesn't worsen things, reboot, or skip the fix.)*

5. **C — Verify full system functionality.** Step 6 confirms the fix and adds preventive measures. *(The other steps come before the fix.)*

6. **False.** Change **one thing at a time**. If you change several at once and it works, you won't know which change fixed it — and you may cause a new problem.

7. **B — it records the problem and fix for next time.** Documentation helps the next person and future you. *(It's not skippable, isn't just overhead, and doesn't replace testing.)*

8. **Matching:** **Identify the problem → B (Step 1)**; **Test the theory → C (Step 3)**; **Document → A (Step 7)**.

9. **Implement.** After the plan of action, you **implement** the solution (or escalate).

10. **Scenario —** You skipped **Step 1, identify the problem** (you didn't gather information or ask what changed). That matters because you may "fix" the wrong thing, miss the real cause, or make it worse — and you have no facts to work from. *(Accept "identify / gather information.")*

### Short answer (`wb-14-01-short-answer.md`) — model answers
1. A method keeps you organized, saves time, and stops you from breaking things that worked; guessing does the opposite.
2. Identify the problem → establish a theory of probable cause → test the theory → establish a plan of action → implement the solution (or escalate) → verify full system functionality → document.
3. It's your best guess at the cause; you **test** it before changing anything.
4. Go back to step 2 and form a new theory — because acting on a wrong theory won't fix the real cause.
5. Escalating is passing the problem to someone with more access, skill, or authority; it's the right call when a problem is beyond you, not a failure.
6. So you can tell exactly which change fixed (or broke) something, and can roll back safely.
7. It records the problem and the fix so the next person (or future you) doesn't repeat the work, and it builds a history.

### Hands-on observation (`wb-14-01-hands-on-observation.md`)

### Vocabulary review (`wb-14-01-vocabulary-review.md`)
- **Troubleshooting methodology** — a fixed set of steps for finding and fixing problems.
- **Theory of probable cause** — your best guess at the cause, tested before changing anything.
- **Escalate** — pass the problem to someone with more access or skill.
- **Verify** — confirm the fix actually worked.
- **Document** — write down the problem, actions, and outcome.
