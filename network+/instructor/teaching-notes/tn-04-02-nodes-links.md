# Teaching Notes — NA-M04-L02 "Nodes, Links, and the Idea of a Connection"

> 🔒 Instructor materials. Pairs with the lesson and `wb-04-02-answers.md`.

## Objective in one line
Learners can define node and link and draw any network as dots and lines.

## Where this sits
Second lesson of Module 04. Gives the two primitives (nodes, links) used to read every diagram in the course. Feeds directly into topologies (04-04). Time: ~20 min + ~40 min worksheets/lab.

## The anchor
The **road map** (cities = nodes, roads = links) and the two-question habit for reading diagrams: "What are the nodes? What are the links?"

## Common misconceptions (and the fix)
- "Only computers are nodes." → Fix: phones, routers, TVs, switches are nodes too.
- "Wireless isn't a real link." → Fix: Wi-Fi is a link; it just uses radio waves.
- Node vs. link mix-up. → Fix: node = a *thing*; link = a *connection*.

## Pacing tips
- The draw-your-network lab is the heart of the lesson and recurs all course — have everyone complete it.
- Keep diagrams simple: router in the middle, add one device at a time.

## How CompTIA tests it
Diagram reading. Spotting nodes and links is the foundational visual skill for the whole exam.

## Support ideas
- Point at real devices and connections around the room and name each as node or link.

## Extension ideas
- Add the internet cloud as a node the router links to.
