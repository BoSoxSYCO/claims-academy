# Teaching Notes — NA-M08-L07 "IPv6: Why It Exists and How to Read It"

> 🔒 Instructor materials. Pairs with the lesson and `wb-08-07-answers.md`.

## Objective in one line
Learners explain why IPv6 exists and read/shorten IPv6 addresses with the two rules, recognizing loopback, link-local, and global unicast.

## Where this sits
Seventh lesson of Module 08. Builds on hex (03-04) and what an IP is (08-01). Second to last in the module; 08-08 closes with public/private + NAT.
Time: ~28 min + ~35 min worksheets/lab.

## The anchor
**Adding digits to phone numbers:** when a city runs out of 7-digit numbers, it adds digits and area codes. IPv4 ran out; IPv6 (128-bit) adds vastly more room. `::` is "etc." for a run of zeros.

## Common misconceptions (and the fix)
- "Drop any zeros." → Fix: only **leading** zeros in a group.
- "Use `::` more than once." → Fix: once only (ambiguity otherwise); compress the **longest** run.
- "fe80 is public." → Fix: fe80:: is **link-local**, not internet-routable.
- "IPv6 has broadcast." → Fix: no broadcast — IPv6 uses multicast.

## Pacing tips
- Do the one shortening example both ways (full → short and short → full).
- The fe80 lab makes it real; note Windows' `%zone` suffix so it doesn't confuse learners.

## Tie-break note (for curious learners)
When two zero runs are equal length, the convention is to compress the **first** one. This lesson's graded items avoid ties (each has a single longest run) so beginners aren't tested on the edge case; mention it only if asked.

## Accuracy note (Prime Directive #7)
The compression example and prefixes are exact. `2001:db8::/32` is the reserved **documentation** range (RFC 3849) — safe to print. `fe80::/10` link-local, `::1` loopback, `::` unspecified, `2000::/3` global unicast, `ff00::/8` multicast are all standard. `ipconfig` is used read-only.

## How CompTIA tests it
128-bit hex format; the two shortening rules; ::1 and fe80::; IPv6 vs IPv4 (no broadcast, huge space). Domain 1.4.

## Support ideas
- Give a printed "drop leading zeros / :: once" card and several practice addresses.

## Extension ideas
- SLAAC and EUI-64 (how a device builds its own address from its MAC) for fast learners.
