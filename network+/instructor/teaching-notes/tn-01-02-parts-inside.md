# Teaching Notes — NA-M01-L02 "The Parts Inside: CPU, RAM, and Storage"

> 🔒 Instructor materials. Pairs with the lesson and `wb-01-02-answers.md`.

## Objective in one line
Learners can tell CPU, RAM, and storage apart and explain the RAM-vs-storage difference.

## Where this sits
Second content lesson. Directly answers the learner's original "I don't know what RAM is." Builds on 01-01's "processing" by revealing what does the processing. Time: ~25 min + ~40 min worksheets/lab.

## The anchor
The **desk analogy**: you = CPU, desktop = RAM, filing cabinet = storage. The single most important image is "**turning the power off wipes the desktop (RAM) but not the cabinet (storage)**." If that lands, the lesson works.

## Common misconceptions (and the fix)
- **RAM = storage.** The #1 confusion. Fix relentlessly with desk vs. cabinet and the power-off wipe.
- **More storage = faster.** Fix: storage holds *more*; RAM lets you *do more at once*. Slowness with many tabs = more RAM.
- **Work is auto-saved.** Fix: RAM is temporary; you must save to storage.

## Pacing tips
- Do the Task Manager lab *live* if possible — watching CPU/RAM move as a browser opens is the payoff.
- Don't introduce bits/bytes math here (that's Module 03). Keep GB as "a size unit."

## How CompTIA tests it
Foundational: network devices are computers with CPU, memory, and storage. Reinforce that framing.

## Support ideas
- Physically act it out: hands = CPU, desk = RAM, drawer = storage; "power off" = sweep the desk clear.

## Extension ideas
- Compare RAM vs. storage on their phone; discuss why phones list both.
