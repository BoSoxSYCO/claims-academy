# Teaching Notes — NA-M04-L03 "LAN, WAN, MAN, PAN, and WLAN Explained"

> 🔒 Instructor materials. Pairs with the lesson and `wb-04-03-answers.md`.

## Objective in one line
Learners match each network name (PAN, LAN, WLAN, MAN, WAN) to its size and a real example, and order them by size.

## Where this sits
Third lesson of Module 04. Builds on nodes/links (04-02): a LAN is nodes and links in one place. Feeds topologies (04-04), which arrange the nodes inside a LAN. Time: ~25 min + ~40 min worksheets/lab.

## The anchor
The **nested rings** picture: rings grow outward from your body (PAN) to the whole world (WAN). Middle letter = size clue: **P**ersonal, **L**ocal, **M**etropolitan, **W**ide.

## Common misconceptions (and the fix)
- "The internet is a big LAN." → Fix: the internet spans the world; it's a WAN.
- "A WLAN is unrelated to a LAN." → Fix: a WLAN *is* a LAN, just over Wi-Fi.
- "MAN and WAN are the same." → Fix: MAN = one city; WAN = across cities/world.
- "PAN doesn't count as a network." → Fix: Bluetooth earbuds are a real PAN.

## Pacing tips
- The find-your-own-examples lab (4.3.E) is the payoff — have everyone complete it on their own phone/laptop.
- Draw the four rings on the board and drop each named example into the right ring.

## How CompTIA tests it
Definition-to-example matching and size ordering. Expect items that give a scenario ("a city links its libraries") and ask for the name.

## Support ideas
- Use the rings-around-you image; place a student's own devices in each ring.

## Extension ideas
- Add bonus terms: **CAN** (Campus Area Network) and **SAN** (Storage Area Network) for fast learners.
