# Teaching Notes — NA-M00-L03 "How to Study So It Sticks"

> 🔒 Instructor material. **Module:** 00 · **Unit:** 1 · **Objective:** orientation · **Time:** ~20 min

---

## Learning objective (one line)

Learners build simple study habits (recall, spacing, notes) that make learning stick.

## Why this lesson matters

Most beginners study by re-reading — the least effective method. Teaching better habits at the very start pays off across all 15 later modules. The three habits (active recall, spaced repetition, notes in your own words) plus short daily sessions are simple, free, and evidence-based. This is the same message as Lesson 15-06, seeded gently here so the whole course is studied well from day one.

## The three habits + rhythm

1. **Active recall** — answer from memory, then check (the workbook and quiz are built for this).
2. **Spaced repetition** — review at growing gaps; the flashcard deck helps later.
3. **Notes** — one or two sentences per lesson, in the learner's own words.
- **Rhythm** — 15–25 focused minutes most days; a one-sentence summary each time; a weekly recall pass.

## Common misconceptions (and fixes)

- **"Re-reading works."** It's passive; recognition fools you. Test yourself.
- **"Familiar means known."** No — cover the page and try to recall first.
- **"Longer sessions are better."** Short and focused beats a marathon.

## Pacing guidance

- ~20 min. The §9 short recall session is the key activity — have them actually try it, misses and all.
- Where learners hesitate: trusting that recall (which feels harder) works better than re-reading (which feels easier).

## How CompTIA tends to test it

The exam rewards recall and application under time pressure — exactly what these habits train. Starting them now builds the muscle early.

## Accuracy note (Prime Directive #7)

The methods are standard and real. Only real course tools are named (workbook, quiz, the `flashcards-core` deck, glossary) — no invented apps or trackers. No new glossary terms: active recall, spaced repetition, and flashcard were already defined in NA-M10-L05 (grepped first to avoid a duplicate); "note-taking" is a plain study habit, not glossarized.

## Extension ideas (fast learners)

- Start a running "weak spots" list to drill with flashcards later (ties to 15-06).
- Experiment with session length to find their best focus window.

## Support ideas (struggling learners)

- Pick just one habit to start (active recall is the highest-value).
- Do the recall session together once before they solo it.
- Reassure: missing points on first recall is normal and useful — it shows what to review.
