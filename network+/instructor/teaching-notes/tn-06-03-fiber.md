# Teaching Notes — NA-M06-L03 "Fiber Optic Basics (Single vs. Multimode)"

> 🔒 Instructor materials. Pairs with the lesson and `wb-06-03-answers.md`.

## Objective in one line
Learners explain fiber (light in a core, cladding reflects) and tell single-mode from multimode, plus why fiber beats copper.

## Where this sits
Third lesson of Module 06. Contrasts with twisted-pair copper (06-01): light vs. electricity, no 100 m limit, no EMI. Feeds wiring standards (06-04) and Ethernet standards (06-05). Time: ~27 min + ~40 min worksheets/lab.

## The anchor
**Flashlight in a mirrored tube.** Light bounces down the core; the cladding is the mirror. Single-mode = narrow straw with one laser beam (far); multimode = wider pipe, many bouncing beams (short).

## Common misconceptions (and the fix)
- "Fiber carries electricity." → Fix: it carries light — that's why no EMI/crosstalk.
- "Single- and multimode swapped." → Fix: single = thin core/laser/far; multi = wide core/LED/short.
- "Bending fiber is fine." → Fix: sharp bends block the light; keep it gently curved.
- "Use fiber everywhere." → Fix: copper is cheaper/fine for short home runs.

## Pacing tips
- The flashlight-in-a-tube lab (6.3.E) makes reflection concrete without any glass.
- Drill the SMF/MMF split by core size and light source; it's the most-tested contrast.

## How CompTIA tests it
SMF vs. MMF (core size, laser vs. LED, distance); fiber's advantages over copper (distance, speed, EMI immunity, security).

## Support ideas
- Name each fiber part as light passes through the paper tube.

## Extension ideas
- Common fiber connectors (LC, SC, ST) and typical jacket colors (yellow SMF, aqua OM3/4) for fast learners.
