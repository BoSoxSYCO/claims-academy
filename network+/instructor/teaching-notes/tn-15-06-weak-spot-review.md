# Teaching Notes — NA-M15-L06 "Weak-Spot Review and Final Flashcard Sprint"

> 🔒 Instructor material. **Module:** 15 · **Unit:** 12 · **Objective:** exam-readiness · **Time:** ~45 min

---

## Learning objective (one line)

Learners turn their weak-domain logs into a focused active-recall sprint with the core flashcards.

## Why this lesson matters

Most learners "study" by re-reading — the least effective method. This lesson replaces that with three high-yield habits: prioritize weak spots, use active recall, and space the repeats. Paired with a real flashcard deck of the most-tested facts, it makes the last study days count. The learner already has the data (two exams' worth of misses by domain); this turns it into a plan.

## The three steps

1. **Prioritize** — rank weak domains by miss-count; target the exact missed facts.
2. **Drill** — flashcards with **active recall** (answer, then check); sort got-it / review-again.
3. **Confirm** — a spot is closed only when answered cold and correctly, twice, spaced.

The **Leitner** boxes (1 daily, 2 every few days, 3 weekly) make spacing concrete.

## The flashcard deck

`assessments/flashcards/flashcards-core.md` — seven decks of high-yield facts, every answer from a taught module: ports (M10), OSI layers (M05), IP/subnet masks/APIPA/DNS/DHCP/ARP (M08), devices/media/VLANs (M06/M07/M11), operations (M12), security CIA/AAA/attacks (M13), and the 7-step method + CLI tools (M14). Port numbers are facts on cards, not glossary headwords.

## Common misconceptions (and fixes)

- **"Re-reading works."** It's passive; recognition fools you. Use recall.
- **"I recognized it, so I know it."** Cover the answer; say it aloud first.
- **"One pass is enough."** Space the repeats; confirm cold, twice.

## Pacing guidance

- ~45 min for the lesson; the flashcard sprint continues daily to exam day.
- Where learners stall: honesty (marking "got it" too easily) and actually spacing repeats.

## How CompTIA tends to test it

The exam demands recall and application under time pressure — exactly what active recall + spaced practice build. This lesson is method, not new content.

## Glossary terms (reused, not new)

The study-method terms **active recall**, **spaced repetition**, and **flashcard** were already introduced in Lesson NA-M10-L05 and are in the master glossary; this lesson reuses and reinforces them. No new glossary terms; footer unchanged (`last: NA-M15-L02`).

## Accuracy note (Prime Directive #7)

Every flashcard answer comes from a taught module. Sample command outputs are varying examples `[TO VERIFY ON HARDWARE]`; no fabricated ports or specs.

## Extension ideas (fast learners)

- Learners write their own cards for any fact they keep missing.
- Build a tiny app or use a spaced-repetition tool with the same decks.

## Support ideas (struggling learners)

- Start with one deck (their weakest) and just Box 1.
- Model one active-recall pass aloud before they solo.
- Anchor the rule: "answer first, then check; space the repeats."
