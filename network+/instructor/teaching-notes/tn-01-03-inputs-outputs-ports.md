# Teaching Notes — NA-M01-L03 "Inputs, Outputs, and Ports on Your Laptop"

> 🔒 Instructor materials. Pairs with the lesson and `wb-01-03-answers.md`.

## Objective in one line
Learners can sort input vs. output devices, name common laptop ports, and find the Ethernet port (or plan an adapter).

## Where this sits
Third content lesson. Turns 01-01's abstract input/output into physical ports, and plants the Ethernet port — the exact port used to build the home lab in Module 06+. Time: ~25 min + ~40 min worksheets/lab.

## The anchor
The **house-openings analogy** (outlet, door, TV jack, data jack) and the rule "**the shape tells you what fits.**"

## Critical real-world note
**Many modern laptops have no built-in Ethernet port.** Do not gloss over this. Have learners check *now*; if missing, they need a **USB-to-Ethernet adapter** before the Module 06 network lab. Catching this early prevents a stall weeks later.

## Common misconceptions (and the fix)
- "All laptops have Ethernet." → Fix: many thin ones don't; adapter solves it.
- Confusing USB with Ethernet slots. → Fix: Ethernet is wider/near-square, often with a clip shape and a network icon.

## Pacing tips
- Spend the time in Section 9 (hands-on). The goal is a learner who can physically point to where the network cable goes.
- Don't get into cable categories/pinouts — that's Module 06.

## How CompTIA tests it
Port/connector recognition by sight. Building visual memory on their own hardware now pays off in Module 06.

## Support ideas
- Provide a printed labeled port photo to hold beside the laptop.

## Extension ideas
- Identify the ports on the home router and the Dell switch too (a gentle preview of Module 07).
