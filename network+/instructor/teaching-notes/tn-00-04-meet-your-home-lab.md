# Teaching Notes — NA-M00-L04 "Meet Your Home Lab — a Tour of Your Equipment"

> 🔒 Instructor material. **Module:** 00 · **Unit:** 1 · **Objective:** orientation · **Time:** ~25 min

---

## Learning objective (one line)

Learners recognize the four pieces of home-lab gear and what each one does.

## Why this lesson matters

The course's second big goal is a real, hands-on home lab. This lesson introduces the gear before any setup, so the first build (from 00-05 on) feels familiar, not intimidating. Naming the four pieces and their jobs — laptop, managed switch, router, cables — gives the learner a map they'll use for the rest of the course. It also connects directly to the exam's switch-vs-router-vs-cable questions.

## The four pieces

1. **Laptop** — the main computer and test tool (runs `ipconfig`, `ping` later).
2. **Dell PowerConnect 3424** — a 24-port **managed switch**; connects wired devices; has a separate **console port**.
3. **Router** — the door to the internet; hands out addresses.
4. **Ethernet cables** — the wires, with **RJ45** connectors; a **link light** shows a live connection.

## Common misconceptions (and fixes)

- **"I must buy everything first."** No — follow on paper; add gear slowly.
- **"The console port is a normal network port."** No — it's management-only, for the first login.
- **"Let's plug it in now."** No — today is look-only; setup starts in 00-05.

## Pacing guidance

- ~25 min. Keep it a relaxed tour. The §9 find-and-name activity is the core.
- Where learners hesitate: the switch can look busy. Reassure that they only need to know its job for now.

## How CompTIA tests it

The exam expects clear switch (Layer 2) vs router (Layer 3) vs cable roles. Meeting the real gear cements those roles far better than text alone.

## Accuracy note (Prime Directive #7)

The Dell PowerConnect 3424 is presented only at a safe, general level — a 24-port managed switch with a console port. Exact port layout, labels, and LED behavior are marked `[TO VERIFY ON HARDWARE]`. Real photos are `[PHOTO NEEDED]` placeholders (per CLAUDE.md §12) — none were fabricated. Command names are real; any output is a varying example `[TO VERIFY ON HARDWARE]`. No new glossary terms — all gear terms were taught earlier and are reused (grepped first).

## Extension ideas (fast learners)

- Label a printed photo of their own gear.
- Look up their exact router model's port layout and note it.

## Support ideas (struggling learners)

- Do the find-and-name activity together, one piece at a time.
- Use the kitchen analogy (§4) to anchor each piece's job.
- Reassure: "You only need to know the four names and jobs right now."
