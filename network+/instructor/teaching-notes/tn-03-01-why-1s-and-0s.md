# Teaching Notes — NA-M03-L01 "Why Computers Only Understand 1s and 0s"

> 🔒 Instructor materials. Pairs with the lesson and `wb-03-01-answers.md`.

## Objective in one line
Learners can explain why computers use two states and define a bit as a single 1 or 0.

## Where this sits
First lesson of Module 03 (Numbers & Binary) — the last on-ramp module before real networking. Binary underlies every IP address, subnet mask, and MAC address in later modules. Time: ~20 min + ~40 min worksheets/lab.

## The anchor
The **light-switch wall**: each switch is on (1) or off (0); a bit is one switch; patterns of bits make all data. Reinforced by the Calculator Programmer-mode lab (numbers → binary).

## Common misconceptions (and the fix)
- "Binary is a different kind of number." → Fix: Calculator shows 5 = 101, the *same* value.
- "A bit can be more than 1/0." → Fix: a switch is only on or off.
- "Letters/pictures aren't binary." → Fix: everything is stored as 1s and 0s.

## Pacing tips
- Keep it conceptual. Counting in binary is 03-02; converting is 03-03. Don't teach place values yet.
- The "byte = 8 bits" is a preview only — one mention is enough.

## How CompTIA tests it
As the foundation for addressing (Domain 1). Every subnetting question rests on this idea.

## Support ideas
- Use real light switches or draw up/down switches; have learners "read" a pattern.

## Extension ideas
- In Calculator, compare 255 (8 bits) vs. 256 (9 bits) and notice when a new bit appears.
