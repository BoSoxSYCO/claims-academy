# Teaching Notes — NA-M06-L01 "Copper Cables: Twisted Pair (Cat5e–Cat8)"

> 🔒 Instructor materials. Pairs with the lesson and `wb-06-01-answers.md`.

## Objective in one line
Learners describe twisted-pair copper cable (4 pairs / 8 wires), explain the twist, tell UTP from STP, and match categories to speed/distance.

## Where this sits
First lesson of Module 06 (Unit 3, physical media). Builds on nodes/links (04-02) and speed words (04-06). Feeds connectors (06-02) and the home-lab cabling. First lesson to require a physical object (E2: an Ethernet cable). Time: ~28 min + ~40 min worksheets/lab.

## The anchor
**Grades of garden hose.** A higher grade carries more, faster — but only up to its rated length (100 m). The twist = braiding that cancels noise.

## Common misconceptions (and the fix)
- "A better cable speeds up any internet." → Fix: the cable sets a ceiling; the plan/gear set the real speed.
- "UTP and STP are interchangeable." → Fix: STP (shielded) for noisy areas; UTP (plain) for most uses.
- "Length doesn't matter." → Fix: 100 m is the standard limit; longer runs get flaky.
- "All Cat cables are the same." → Fix: higher number = higher top speed.

## Pacing tips
- The read-a-real-cable lab (6.1.E) is the payoff and ties straight to the home lab — pass around cables.
- Drill the category→speed table; it's the most-tested fact.

## How CompTIA tests it
Category → speed/distance; UTP vs. STP; the 100 m twisted-pair limit; crosstalk/interference terms.

## Support ideas
- Have learners physically find the printed CAT text on a cable in the room.

## Extension ideas
- Plenum vs. riser jacket ratings; solid vs. stranded conductors (patch vs. in-wall) for fast learners.
