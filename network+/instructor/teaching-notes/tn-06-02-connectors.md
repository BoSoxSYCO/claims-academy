# Teaching Notes — NA-M06-L02 "Connectors and Jacks (RJ45, Keystones)"

> 🔒 Instructor materials. Pairs with the lesson and `wb-06-02-answers.md`.

## Objective in one line
Learners tell connector from jack, identify RJ45 (8 pins, tab) vs. the smaller RJ11, and name keystone/patch-panel/patch-cable parts.

## Where this sits
Second lesson of Module 06. Builds on twisted-pair copper (06-01) — the 8 wires map to the RJ45's 8 pins. Feeds fiber (06-03) and the home-lab cabling. Time: ~26 min + ~40 min worksheets/lab.

## The anchor
**Plug and outlet.** Connector = plug (male); jack = socket (female). One plug shape fits one socket, and the "click" means it's seated.

## Common misconceptions (and the fix)
- "RJ45 = RJ11." → Fix: RJ45 is wider with 8 pins (Ethernet); RJ11 is smaller (phone).
- "Connector and jack are the same." → Fix: connector = plug; jack = socket.
- "Just yank the cable out." → Fix: press the locking tab first, or it breaks.
- "A broken tab is harmless." → Fix: it causes a loose, blinking link — a top physical fault.

## Pacing tips
- The seat-an-RJ45 lab (6.2.E) is the payoff and ties straight to the home lab — everyone should feel the click.
- Pass around a real RJ45 and RJ11 side by side for the size difference.

## How CompTIA tests it
Connector identification from a picture; RJ45 = 8 pins/Ethernet, RJ11 = phone; keystone/patch-panel terms.

## Support ideas
- Let learners physically press a tab and feel it flex before seating a cable.

## Extension ideas
- Show a crimp tool and a keystone punch-down (demo only — building comes later) for fast learners.
