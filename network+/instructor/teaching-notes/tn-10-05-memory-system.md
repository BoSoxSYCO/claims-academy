# 🔒 Teaching Notes — Lesson 10-05: A Memory System for the Port Table

> Instructor guide. Written so a substitute could teach this cold.

## Learning objective (one line)
Learners apply four habits — chunking, active recall, spaced repetition, and mnemonics — to memorize the must-know port table.

## Where this sits
- **Before:** 10-01 through 10-04 (all the ports and their secure twins). This is the **capstone** of Module 10.
- **After:** Module 11 (network implementation — VLANs, routing, wireless).
- This lesson teaches **study method**, not new ports. Keep the port facts accurate but let the technique be the star.

## The four habits (the spine of the lesson)
1. **Chunking** — five job groups (web, mail, remote, files, services).
2. **Active recall** — cover, say, check; both directions.
3. **Spaced repetition** — today, +1, +2, +4, +7 days.
4. **Mnemonics** — only for the ports that keep slipping.

## Common misconceptions (correct both live)
1. **"Rereading is studying."** It builds recognition, not recall. Prove it: have learners reread once, then close the book and quiz cold — the gap is the lesson.
2. **"Recognizing the answer = knowing it."** On a multiple-choice practice item they may pick right by elimination but blank on a fill-in. Push covered-up recall.

## Pacing (~30 min)
- 5 min — the phone-number analogy and why recall beats rereading.
- 8 min — walk the four habits.
- 15 min — build the 16-card deck and run round 1 (this is the heart of the lesson).
- 2 min — write the spaced schedule on a real calendar.
Learners resist active recall because it feels hard. Name that feeling as the point — "hard means it's working."

## How CompTIA tends to test it
- The exam tests the **ports** (1.5), both directions and TCP/UDP. This lesson is the method that gets them there.
- No exam item asks "what is spaced repetition" — but the technique is what makes the port items automatic.

## Support ideas (struggling learners)
- Start with one job group (web: 80/443) and only add a group once the last sticks.
- Provide pre-made cards for learners who struggle to write them.
- Sit with them for one covered-up round so they feel the difference.

## Extension ideas (fast learners)
- Introduce the **Leitner box** (3-pile spaced system): missed cards move to the daily pile, mastered cards to the weekly pile.
- Have them build cards for the secure twins (10-04) too.

## Accuracy note (Prime Directive #7)
Every port fact reused here is standards-based (from 10-03/10-04). Active recall and spaced repetition are well-established learning-science techniques. Learners' quiz scores are their own — no fabricated data.
