# Teaching Notes — NA-M05-L01 "Why We Need a Model of a Network"

> 🔒 Instructor materials. Pairs with the lesson and `wb-05-01-answers.md`.

## Objective in one line
Learners explain what a model and a layer are, give three benefits of layering, and name the two models (OSI 7, TCP/IP 4).

## Where this sits
First lesson of Module 05 and the mental frame for the whole exam. Builds on nodes/links (04-02) and speed words (04-06). Feeds the OSI tour (05-02) and TCP/IP (05-03). Deliberately motivates WHY before any layer names appear. Time: ~22 min + ~40 min worksheets/lab.

## The anchor
**A delivery company split into departments** — each has one job and hands off to the next; a late package means you ask "which department?" That maps directly to layers and troubleshoot-by-layer.

## Common misconceptions (and the fix)
- "A model shows every detail." → Fix: a model hides detail on purpose (like a subway map).
- "Layers work alone." → Fix: each layer hands data to the next; they're a stack.
- "OSI vs. TCP/IP — pick the right one." → Fix: both describe the same networking; they split it differently.
- "Just memorize the layers." → Fix: the point is dividing work and finding problems.

## Pacing tips
- The split-a-task lab (5.1.E) is the payoff and previews OSI — have everyone do it.
- Keep layer names generic here (app/address/move/signal); real names come in 05-02.

## How CompTIA tests it
The entire exam is layer-organized. This lesson builds the frame; expect later questions to hinge on "which layer."

## Support ideas
- Reuse the delivery-departments picture for every question a learner raises.

## Extension ideas
- Have fast learners guess how their invented layers map to real OSI layers (checked next lesson).
