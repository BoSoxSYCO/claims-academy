# [Module 15 · Lesson 06] Weak-Spot Review and Final Flashcard Sprint

> **Unit:** Unit 12 — Capstone & Exam Readiness · **Time:** ~45 min · **Equipment:** E1 (none; the flashcards)
> **You'll need first:** your weak-domain logs from Lessons 15-04 and 15-05.

You have two practice exams behind you. You know your weak spots. Now let's close them. This lesson does two things. It shows you how to review your weak domains the smart way — not by re-reading everything. And it gives you a compact flashcard deck of the most-tested facts, so your last days of study hit hard.

---

## 1. Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- [ ] Rank your weak spots and review the highest-value ones first.
- [ ] Use **active recall** and **spaced repetition** with the flashcards.
- [ ] Know when a weak spot is truly closed.
- [ ] Run a focused final sprint instead of re-reading everything.

---

## 2. Introduction — why this matters

Re-reading a whole module feels productive. It mostly is not. Your eyes slide over words you already recognize, and the hard parts stay hard. Time runs out and the weak spots remain.

Smart review is different. You spend your time only where you are weak. And you practice by *pulling* the answer from memory, not by re-reading it. That is how facts stick.

You already have the data: your missed questions from two exams, tagged by domain. This lesson turns that list into a short, sharp plan.

🎯 **Exam tip:** The exam rewards recall, not recognition. Practice recall, and the exam feels familiar.

🔧 **Lab link:** Your "lab" here is the flashcard deck — a real tool you'll use daily until exam day.

---

## 3. Simple explanation

Three steps: **Prioritize**, **Drill**, **Confirm**. Then repeat until your weak spots close.

### Step 1 — Prioritize

- List your weak domains from both exams (most misses first).
- Pick the top one or two. Ignore what you already know.
- Within a domain, target the exact facts you missed.

### Step 2 — Drill with the flashcards

- Use `assessments/flashcards/flashcards-core.md`.
- **Active recall:** read the question, answer from memory, *then* check.
- Sort each card: "got it" or "review again."
- **Spaced repetition:** re-drill the "review again" pile after a day, then a few days later.

### Step 3 — Confirm it's closed

A weak spot is closed when you can answer it cold, correctly, twice, spaced apart. Not once. Not while looking. Cold and repeated.

### The Leitner idea (simple boxes)

- Box 1 = missed cards (review daily).
- Box 2 = got it once (review every few days).
- Box 3 = got it twice, spaced (review weekly).
- A card moves up when you get it, and back to Box 1 if you miss it.

> **The big idea:** Don't re-read everything. Prioritize your weak spots, drill them with active recall, space the repeats, and confirm each is closed by answering cold — twice.

💡 **Tip:** Short daily sprints beat one long cram. Fifteen focused minutes a day wins.

⚠️ **Watch out:** Recognizing an answer is not knowing it. Always answer *before* you uncover the card.

---

## 4. Real-world analogy

> **"Study like a gym plan — train your weakest muscle, not your strongest."**

| Smart gym plan | Smart review |
|----------------|--------------|
| Work the weak muscle | Drill the weak domain |
| Lift, don't just watch | Recall, don't just re-read |
| Rest, then repeat | Space your repeats |
| Add weight when ready | Move a card up when it's solid |

Nobody gets stronger by watching others lift. You get stronger by lifting — and by training what is weak.

## 5. Vocabulary

| Term | Plain-language meaning |
|------|------------------------|
| **Active recall** | Pulling an answer from memory before checking — the strongest way to learn. |
| **Spaced repetition** | Reviewing a fact again after growing gaps of time, so it sticks. |
| **Flashcard** | A card with a question on one side and the answer on the other. |
| **Baseline** | Your earlier score/weak-spots to measure improvement against. |

## 9. Hands-on lab

**Goal:** run one full weak-spot sprint using the core flashcards.
**Why:** a focused sprint closes weak spots faster than re-reading modules.

**You will need**
- Your weak-domain logs (15-04, 15-05), `assessments/flashcards/flashcards-core.md`, and the self-quiz log. ~30 min.

⚠️ **Before you start:** answer every card *before* you uncover it. Recognizing is not recalling.

### 9a. Step-by-step instructions

1. From your two logs, write your top two weak domains.
2. Open the matching flashcard deck(s) for those domains.
3. For each card: read the Q, say your answer aloud, then check.
4. Mark each card "got it" or "review again" on `wb-15-06-hands-on-observation.md`.
5. Set the "review again" pile aside. Re-drill it after a day.
6. A card is done when you answer it cold and correctly twice, spaced.

### 9b. Expected results

- A self-quiz log showing which cards you got and which need another pass.

- ✅ **You did it if:** you answered from memory first and sorted every card honestly.

> ⚠️ Any command output on a card is a varying example. **[TO VERIFY ON HARDWARE]**

### 9c. Verify it worked

1. Did you answer before uncovering each card?
2. Is your "review again" pile scheduled for tomorrow?

### 9d. Reset / roll back

Nothing to undo. Keep your card piles and repeat daily until Box 1 is empty.

> 🔧 **Home-lab continuity:** carry your remaining "review again" cards into the 7-day plan (Lesson 15-07).

## 10. Common mistakes

| ⚠️ Mistake | What happens | How to fix / avoid it |
|-----------|--------------|-----------------------|
| Re-reading whole modules | Time lost, weak spots remain | Drill only your weak domains |
| Peeking before answering | You practice recognition, not recall | Answer first, then check |
| Cramming once | Facts fade fast | Space your repeats over days |
| Calling a card "done" too soon | It's not really closed | Require cold + correct + twice |

---

## 11. Troubleshooting

| The worry | What to do |
|-----------|------------|
| "Too many weak areas." | Pick the top one or two; ignore the rest for now |
| "I keep missing the same card." | Keep it in Box 1; drill it daily until it sticks |
| "I recognize but can't recall." | Cover the answer; say it aloud before checking |
| "No time for long study." | Do 15 focused minutes daily — spacing beats cramming |
