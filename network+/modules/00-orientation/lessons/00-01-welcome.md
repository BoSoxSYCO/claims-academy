## 2. Introduction — why this matters

You use networks every day, even if you have never heard the word. When your phone loads a video, that is a network. When your TV streams a show, that is a network. When you send a message, it travels across a network to reach a friend.

Networks are the quiet system behind modern life. Learning how they work is a real, useful skill. It can lead to a job, more confidence, and the ability to fix things yourself.

This first lesson has one job: to welcome you and show you where we are going. No hard words. No pressure. Just a clear map of the road ahead.

🎯 **Exam tip:** You do not need to memorize anything yet. Today is only about the big picture.

🔧 **Lab link:** Later, you will build a small network at home with real equipment. For now, just picture your own devices talking to each other.

## 3. Simple explanation

Let's meet three ideas gently: a network, the certification, and this course.

### What is a network?

A **network** is two or more devices connected so they can share and talk. That is the whole idea. Your phone, laptop, and TV at home form a small network. They share one internet connection and can reach each other.

Think of a network like a group of friends passing notes. Each friend is a device. The notes are the messages. The rules for passing notes fairly are what we will learn.

### What is CompTIA Network+?

A **certification** is a credential you earn by passing an exam. It proves you have a skill.

The **CompTIA Network+** is an entry-level certification for networking. "Entry-level" means it is made for beginners. It is also vendor-neutral, which means it teaches ideas that work with any brand of equipment — not just one company's. The current exam version is called N10-009.

Passing it tells an employer: "This person understands how networks work."

### What will this course do?

This course has three promises:

1. **Teach the concepts** — every term, slowly, with pictures and examples.
2. **Build a real home lab** — you will connect real gear with your own hands.
3. **Get you exam-ready** — with practice, quizzes, and a clear study plan.

> **The big idea:** A network is just devices talking to each other. Network+ is a beginner certification that proves you understand them. This course teaches the ideas, builds a real lab, and gets you ready for the exam — starting from zero.

💡 **Tip:** Go at your own pace. This course waits for you. There is no clock ticking.

⚠️ **Watch out:** Don't compare yourself to anyone else. Everyone starts as a beginner, including every expert you admire.

## 9. Hands-on lab

**Goal:** notice the network you already have around you.
**Why:** networks feel less scary once you see you already live with one.

**You will need**
- Just your eyes and, if you like, a pen and paper. ~10 min.

⚠️ **Before you start:** you will not touch any settings. This is only looking and noticing.

### 9a. Step-by-step instructions

1. Look around your home. List the devices that connect to the internet.
2. Note the box that shares the internet (often called a router or a modem).
3. Draw a simple picture: the internet at the top, your router in the middle, your devices at the bottom.
4. Count how many devices share your one internet connection.

### 9b. Expected results

- A short list of your devices and a simple drawing of your home network.

- ✅ **You did it if:** you can point to the device that shares the internet, and name a few things connected to it.

> ⚠️ Every home is different. Your list is your own — there is no wrong answer here.

### 9c. Verify it worked

1. Can you name at least three devices on your home network?
2. Can you point to the box that shares the internet?

### 9d. Reset / roll back

Nothing to undo — you only looked and drew. Keep your drawing; it's a nice start.

> 🔧 **Home-lab continuity:** later, you'll build a lab that works just like this drawing, with real cables.
