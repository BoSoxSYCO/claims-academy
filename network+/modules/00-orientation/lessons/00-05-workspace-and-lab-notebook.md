## 2. Introduction — why this matters

A messy, cramped space leads to mistakes. Cables get yanked. Drinks get spilled. Frustration grows. A little setup up front prevents all of that.

Safety matters too. Some gear is shared, and one wrong move can lock other people out. Simple habits keep you and the equipment safe.

A lab notebook is the quiet hero of learning by doing. When something works — or breaks — your notes tell you exactly what happened. That record saves hours later.

🎯 **Exam tip:** Good documentation habits are tested on the exam, and they start right here with your notebook.

🔧 **Lab link:** This is the setup for every hands-on lab in the course. Do it once, use it always.

## 3. Simple explanation

Three simple things: a good spot, safe habits, and a notebook.

### Set up a good spot

- Pick a place with a power outlet nearby and a little room to spread out.
- Keep your gear together — laptop, switch, router, and cables in one area.
- Keep cables tidy. Loose cables get pulled and cause confusion.
- Good light helps you read port labels and see the small **link light**.

### Work safely

A few habits protect you and the gear:

- Don't force a connector. An **RJ45** clicks in gently; forcing it bends the pins.
- Power down before you move cables around, when you can.
- Keep food and drinks away from the equipment.
- On a shared **switch**, do not erase settings or change ports you weren't told to use. A wrong change can lock others out. *[TO VERIFY ON HARDWARE — follow any rules for your specific shared unit.]*

### Start a lab notebook

A lab notebook is your record of every hands-on session. Paper or digital both work. It is a simple form of **documentation**, the same habit used across real networks.

For each session, write:

- The date and which lesson or lab you're doing.
- What you did — the steps, in order.
- What you saw — link lights, command results, anything odd.

This ties back to the note-taking habit from Lesson 00-03. Keep one notebook for the whole course.

### A quick pre-lab checklist

Before each hands-on session, check:

1. Power outlet reachable, space clear.
2. Gear and cables together and tidy.
3. Notebook open, with today's date written.
4. You know which lesson or lab you're doing.

> **The big idea:** A tidy, safe workspace and a simple lab notebook make every hands-on lab smoother. Pick a good spot, work safely (never force a connector, protect a shared switch), and write down the date, what you did, and what you saw.

💡 **Tip:** Label your cables and ports with tape and a pen. "Port 1," "to router" — small labels save big confusion.

⚠️ **Watch out:** The shared switch is easy to disrupt. When unsure, only add your own cable and change nothing else.

## 4. Real-world analogy

> **"A lab notebook is like a cook's recipe notes — write down what worked so you can do it again."**

| A cook's notes | Your lab notebook |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Clean, clear counter | Tidy, safe workspace |
| "Added salt, tasted better" | "Plugged port 1, light turned green" |
| Notes what burned | Notes what broke |
| Can repeat the dish | Can repeat (or fix) the setup |

Great cooks keep notes. So do great technicians. Your notebook is that habit.

## 9. Hands-on lab

**Goal:** set up your workspace and start your lab notebook.
**Why:** this is the foundation for every hands-on lab you'll do in this course.

**You will need**
- Your gear or paper, a notebook (paper or digital), tape, and a pen. ~20 min.

⚠️ **Before you start:** you're arranging and writing, not configuring. Don't change any switch settings.

### 9a. Step-by-step instructions

1. Pick your spot: near power, with room and good light.
2. Group your gear together and tidy the cables.
3. Open your notebook. Write today's date at the top.
4. Make three columns: "What I did," "What I saw," and leave room for notes.
5. Label two cables with tape (for example, "to router" and "to laptop").
6. Run the pre-lab checklist (outlet, space, notebook, which lab).
7. Write one line in your notebook: "Set up my workspace and notebook."

### 9b. Expected results

- A tidy, safe workspace and a notebook with today's date and three columns.

- ✅ **You did it if:** your gear is grouped safely and your notebook is started.

> ⚠️ Follow any specific rules for your shared switch. When unsure, change nothing on it. **[TO VERIFY ON HARDWARE]**

### 9c. Verify it worked

1. Is your gear grouped near power, with tidy cables?
2. Does your notebook have today's date and three columns?

### 9d. Reset / roll back

Nothing to undo — you arranged and wrote, nothing was configured. Keep your setup for Module 01.

> 🔧 **Home-lab continuity:** this workspace and notebook carry through the whole course. Every later lab uses them.

## 10. Common mistakes

| ⚠️ Mistake | What happens | How to fix / avoid it |
|-----------|--------------|-----------------------|
| Cramped, messy space | Cables pulled, mistakes made | Pick a spot with room; tidy the cables |
| Forcing a connector | Bent pins, no link | The RJ45 clicks gently; never force it |
| Skipping the notebook | You forget what you did | Write date, what you did, what you saw |
| Changing a shared switch | You lock others out | Only touch what you were told to |

---

## 11. Troubleshooting

| The worry | What to do |
|-----------|------------|
| "I have no dedicated space." | A cleared corner of a table works; just keep it tidy |
| "Paper or digital notebook?" | Either — pick what you'll actually use |
| "What if I break something?" | Follow the safety rules; don't force or erase anything |
| "Do I really need labels?" | They save real confusion once cables multiply — yes, use them |
